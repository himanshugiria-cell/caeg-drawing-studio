01

HP decides the front view.

Above HP → front view above XY.
Below HP → front view below XY.
On HP → front view on XY.

02

VP decides the top view.

In front of VP → top view below XY.
Behind VP → top view above XY.
On VP → top view on XY.

HP is the horizontal plane. VP is the vertical plane. They meet at XY. These rules show the drawing after HP rotates downward into the same sheet as VP.

MANUAL CONSTRUCTION / WORKED EXAMPLE P

30 above. 40 in front.

P is 30 mm above HP
and 40 mm in front of VP.

  1. The reference line

    Draw XY horizontally. Choose a convenient horizontal position and draw one light projector perpendicular to XY. The question gives no horizontal spacing.

  2. The front view p′

    Read the HP information. Measure 30 mm upward from XY on the projector. Mark p′.

  3. The top view p

    Read the VP information. In front of VP becomes below XY after unfolding. Measure 40 mm downward from XY on the same projector. Mark p.

  4. The finished pair

    Label both views and show 30 and 40 from XY. Check the perpendicular alignment. The 70 mm separation of the two views is the sum of these two distances.

Can the two views coincide away from XY?

Yes. A point 20 mm above HP and 20 mm behind VP has both views 20 mm above XY. Equal distances in the fourth quadrant also give coincidence below XY. A point on both planes has both views coincident on XY.

AUTOCAD / THE SAME 2D DRAWING

Point P at the command line.

A teacher-added workflow
for the manual’s AutoCAD requirement.

Use a new 2D drawing, World UCS and the Top view. Let one drawing unit represent one millimetre. Set DYNMODE to 0 so these command-line coordinates are absolute.

Set PDMODE to 3 and PDSIZE to 2 for visible point markers. If necessary, use REGEN to refresh the display.

  1. XY and projector

    Run LINE. Enter 0,0 and 200,0 for XY, then Enter to finish. Run LINE again with 40,-50 and 40,50 for the projector, then Enter.

  2. The two points

    Run POINT and enter 40,30 for p′. Run POINT again and enter 40,-40 for p. Both use the same x coordinate, 40.

  3. Labels and dimensions

    Use TEXT or MTEXT to add X, Y, p′ and p. Show the 30 and 40 mm distances from XY. Save your DWG file.

Here y = 0 represents XY on the flat sheet. The horizontal x = 40 is a convenient layout choice. These are 2D drawing coordinates, rather than the physical 3D coordinates of P.

Command references: Autodesk, drawing lines; PDMODE and point display.

CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

A short pause before the next drawing.

A point lies on VP and 20 mm below HP. Which view lies on XY?

Its top view lies on XY because the point is on VP. Its front view lies 20 mm below XY because it is below HP.

What is the difference between p′ and p?

p′ is the front view, located using the HP information. p is the top view, located using the VP information. They lie on the same projector perpendicular to XY.

Does the higher mark always mean the front view?

No. For Q, both views lie above XY and q, the top view, is higher. Use the prime mark to identify the front view.
