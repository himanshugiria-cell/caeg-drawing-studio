EXPERIMENT 08 / SEMESTER I 2026–27

Two endpoints.
One line.

You already know how to project a point. A line simply has two endpoints: project A, project B, then join their matching views.

Build the idea with the eight numerical examples in your official lab manual. Discover when a view shows the true length—and when it becomes shorter or even a point.

READ THE POSITION BEFORE DRAWING

Parallel, perpendicular, inclined.

Each view removes one component
of the line’s position.

Parallel to a plane

The projection on that plane shows the true length. A line parallel to VP has a true-length front view. A line parallel to HP has a true-length top view.

Perpendicular to a plane

The projection on that plane is a point: the two endpoints overlap. The other view shows the true length perpendicular to XY.

Inclined to both planes

Both views are shorter than the true length. The angles of these views with XY are apparent angles; they generally differ from the given true angles.

θ and φ belong to the line in space. θ is the true angle with HP; φ is the true angle with VP. α is the angle of the front view with XY; β is the angle of the top view with XY.

For the examples with B rising and moving farther in front: Δz = TL sin θ, Δy = TL sin φ, FV = TL cos φ, and TV = TL cos θ. The remaining horizontal change is Δx = √(TL² − Δy² − Δz²).

A real line requires sin² θ + sin² φ ≤ 1. Equality gives Δx = 0; its nonzero projections are perpendicular to XY. A view that collapses to a point has no direction angle.

WORKED EXAMPLE / MANUAL FIG. 8.4

80 mm. 30° to HP.
Parallel to VP.

A is 20 mm above HP
and 40 mm in front of VP.

  1. Draw XY and locate A.

    Draw one light projector. Mark a′ 20 mm above XY and a 40 mm below XY.

  2. Use the true-length view.

    The line is parallel to VP, so its front view shows TL. From a′ draw a′b′ = 80 mm at 30° above a horizontal line through a′. Label the far end b′.

  3. Project down to find b.

    Draw a light projector through b′. Because AB is parallel to VP, B is also 40 mm in front of VP. Draw a horizontal locus through a; its intersection with the new projector is b.

  4. Join and check.

    Join a to b. The top view is horizontal and approximately 69.28 mm long. The front view is 80 mm long at 30° to XY. B is 60 mm above HP.

A: 20 mm above HP, 30 mm in front of VP.

2 / ROTATE PLAN & PROJECT UPFINAL TWO VIEWS

  1. Locate A in both views.

    Draw XY. Mark a′ 20 mm above it and a 30 mm below it, on one projector.

  2. Temporarily make AB parallel to VP.

    From a′ draw a′b₁′ = 70 mm at the given θ = 45°. Project b₁′ down to the horizontal through a, obtaining b₁. This gives the initial top-view length ab₁ = 70 cos 45° ≈ 49.50 mm and B’s height of 69.50 mm above HP.

  3. Find the required depth change.

    In a separate right triangle, draw a 70 mm hypotenuse at φ = 30° to a horizontal. Its vertical rise is 70 sin 30° = 35 mm. This represents B’s extra distance in front of VP, not the angle of the final top view.

  4. Rotate the top view onto B’s depth locus.

    B must be 30 + 35 = 65 mm in front of VP. Draw its locus 65 mm below XY. With a as centre and radius ab₁ ≈ 49.50 mm, draw an arc to meet this locus on the right. Mark the intersection b. Draw ab. Keep the radius unchanged during the rotation.

  5. Recover the final front view.

    Project vertically upward from b. Through b₁′ draw a horizontal height locus. Their intersection is b′. Join a′b′. The front and top endpoints of B must share the same projector.

  6. Check the result.

    The horizontal separation is 35 mm. FV ≈ 60.62 mm at α ≈ 54.74° to XY. TV ≈ 49.50 mm at β = 45° to XY. Neither view is 70 mm long. The true inclinations remain θ = 45° and φ = 30°.

Do not draw the final front view directly at 45° and the final top view at 30°. Those are the line’s true inclinations to the planes. The final view angles are found by construction.

What if a line lies on both HP and VP?

It lies along their intersection, XY. Both its views coincide on XY and both show the true length. This extra position is part of the topic; the manual’s eight numerical problems do not give a separate example for it.

THE LINE MAY NEED TO BE EXTENDED

Where does it meet a plane?

A trace is an intersection
of the line, extended if necessary, with a reference plane.

Horizontal trace H

Extend the front view to meet XY at h′. Draw a perpendicular projector from h′ to meet the extended top view at h. H is on HP: its front view lies on XY.

Vertical trace V

Extend the top view to meet XY at v. Project from v to meet the extended front view at v′. V is on VP: its top view lies on XY.

A line parallel to a plane and outside it has no finite trace on that plane. A line lying in a plane has no single, unique trace there. If a projected view is a point, do not try to extend that point into a line; use the line’s known perpendicular position to identify the trace.

Keep projectors on a thin construction layer.

Use World UCS and the Top view. Set DYNMODE to 0 before entering these absolute command-line coordinates. These are positions on the flat drawing sheet; they are not physical 3D endpoint coordinates.

  1. Draw XY and the projectors.

    Use LINE from 0,0 to 180,0. Finish the command with Enter. Draw separate construction lines from 40,-55 to 40,75 and from 109.282,-55 to 109.282,75.

  2. Draw the front view.

    Use LINE from a′ at 40,20 to b′ at 109.282,60. This is approximately 80 mm long at 30° to XY.

PENCIL-AND-PAPER PRACTICE

Draw before checking.

Extra practice problems.
No submission date or marking scheme is set here.

  1. A 60 mm line PQ is parallel to HP and VP. P is 30 mm above HP and 20 mm in front of VP. Draw its two views.
  2. An 80 mm line RS is 30° to HP and parallel to VP. R is 15 mm above HP and 25 mm in front of VP. Choose S above and to the right of R.
  3. A 60 mm line TU is 30° to VP and parallel to HP. T is 25 mm above HP and 20 mm in front of VP. Choose U farther in front of VP and to the right of T.
Check the three answers

1. FV = TV = 60 mm. Both views are horizontal: FV 30 mm above XY and TV 20 mm below it.

2. FV = 80 mm at 30°; TV ≈ 69.28 mm, horizontal. S is 55 mm above HP and 25 mm in front of VP.

3. FV ≈ 51.96 mm, horizontal; TV = 60 mm at 30° downward from the horizontal. U is 25 mm above HP and 50 mm in front of VP.

Optional challenge: inclined to both planes

TL = 100 mm, θ = 30°, φ = 30°. A is 20 mm above HP and 20 mm in front of VP. Choose B higher, farther in front and to the right.

Check after drawing: Δx ≈ 70.71 mm, Δy = Δz = 50 mm. FV = TV ≈ 86.60 mm. Both apparent angles are approximately 35.26°, rather than 30°.

Five quick viva questions

What is TL? The actual length of the line in space.

When is the FV a point? When the line is perpendicular to VP.

When does TV show TL? When the line is parallel to HP, including a line lying in HP.

Does an apparent angle always equal a given true inclination? No; equality occurs in appropriate special positions.

Which view of the horizontal trace lies on XY? Its front view h′.

For your record: title, aim, given data, labelled views, thin projectors, dimensions and a short result.
